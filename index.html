How the lipids of neuronal membranes shape the receptors embedded in them.

01

What we do

Most of the receptors neurons use to talk to each other sit inside a membrane, and that membrane is not a passive backdrop. Its lipid composition differs between cell types and between organelles, and it shifts during development, ageing and disease. Those shifts reach the receptor.

We use molecular dynamics simulations — coarse-grained and all-atom, with enhanced sampling and, increasingly, machine learning — to work out how. The systems we care about most are G protein-coupled receptors in brain membranes, and the lipids we keep coming back to are cholesterol and the polyunsaturated fatty acids that make neuronal membranes unusual.

We are also bringing new technologies into every one of our lines of work: deep learning and generative models trained on simulation data, AI-based structure prediction, and GPU-accelerated sampling that reaches system sizes and timescales that were out of reach only a few years ago.

Almost everything we do is done alongside experimental groups, so the predictions get tested rather than accumulated.

02

Lines of work

Mechanism

Lipids as direct regulators of receptor structure

We found that cholesterol can leave the bilayer and enter the interior of a GPCR, occupying cavities that overlap with ligand-binding regions. More recently we showed that polyunsaturated lipids reshape the dynamics of the dopamine D2 receptor and bias its signalling. The open question is how general this is: which receptors read the lipidome, through which residues, and with what functional consequence.

Context

Receptors in their native environment

The membranes neurons are built from vary from one cell to another and from one place in the cell to another. We build physiologically realistic membrane models so that what we learn about a receptor holds in the environment where it actually works.

Methods and data

Tools other people can use

Simulation data is only useful if it can be inspected, compared and reused. We have contributed to GPCRmd, the reference open platform for GPCR simulations, and to software for membrane analysis and for sharing trajectories on the web. Anything we generate goes out under FAIR principles.

Tools and resources

  • GPCRmdPlatform

    Open web platform for depositing, visualising and analysing molecular dynamics simulations of GPCRs. Developed with the Selent group.

  • MEMBPLUGINVMD plugin

    VMD plugin for the analysis of membrane properties — order parameters, thickness, area per lipid and more.

  • MDsrvServer

    Lightweight server for streaming and sharing molecular dynamics trajectories directly in a browser.
